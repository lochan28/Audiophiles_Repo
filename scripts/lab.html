Runs Bench Lab

1 · Choose a voice

Any format your browser can play, any length up to two minutes. It is decoded and resampled to 16 kHz mono in the browser, so nothing about codecs reaches the server. Noise clips are repeated as many times as needed to cover the whole recording, however long it is.
Every clip sits in a 3-second window. Shorter clips are placed inside it, and the SNR below is measured only over the part where someone is actually speaking — not over the silence.

2 · Pile on the noise

Each layer is set to its own SNR against the speech. Stacking layers therefore makes the mixture noisier than any single number suggests — four layers at 5 dB land near −1 dB, because noise powers add. The real combined figure is shown once you mix.

3 · Mix and enhance

4 · Live from the microphone

Speak into the microphone and hear the model clean it as you go. Added delay is about 230 ms: 100 ms to fill a chunk and 128 ms the model holds back before a sample is final. Use headphones — on speakers the cleaned output feeds back into the microphone and the model starts fighting its own echo. The A/B button swaps between the raw microphone and the processed stream without a gap, so the difference is the only thing that changes.
